01 — Research

What I work on

Four areas, one question: how cheaply can a large linear system be solved — in theory, and on real machines?

Electronic Design Automation

Making SPICE-class circuit simulation tractable: preconditioned iterative solvers, graph sparsification and self-tuning linear-algebra kernels that adapt to the matrix they are given.

  • Circuit simulation
  • PCG / GMRES
  • Spectral sparsification

High-Performance Computing

Randomized and parallel algorithms for sparse linear systems at scale: sketching to cut asymptotic cost, nested-dissection scheduling to expose parallelism, and implementations that stay accurate on the hard matrices.

  • Randomized linear algebra
  • Parallel scheduling
  • Sparse factorisation

Theoretical Computer Science

The complexity of sketching and sparsification — implicit dense sketches, their lower bounds, and what can be assumed away once randomness is allowed. One line of this work appeared as a first-author paper in Information and Computation.

  • Sketching complexity
  • Lower bounds
  • Randomized algorithms

Machine Learning

Numerical foundations for learning — second-order and randomized optimisation methods, and the linear algebra that decides whether they converge.

  • Randomized optimisation
  • Numerical stability
  • Scientific ML
